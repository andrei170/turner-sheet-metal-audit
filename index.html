Finding 1 — There Is Nowhere For A Technician To Land
Pages on turnersheetmetal.com: 4 (Home, Heating, Cooling, 24/7 Emergency) We checked for a careers page at ten addresses: /careers/ /jobs/ /employment/ /join-our-team/ /apply/ /hiring/ /work-with-us/ /team/ /about/ /contact/ Every one: not found
Good technicians look you up before they apply

Somebody weighing up a move checks what kind of firm you are. Right now they find four pages about furnaces and air conditioners, and nothing about the work, the truck, the pay or the people. There is no contact form on the site either, so even a willing applicant has to find the number and ring during the day.

This is the number to decide before you advertise anything

A technician in your area is being offered between $18 and $40 an hour right now, and one commercial employer 45 miles away is publishing $80,000 a year. If your rate is not in that range, no advert fixes it. If it is, saying the number out loud is the single biggest thing you can do — most local adverts do not.

There are only 3,690 people in the whole of Kansas who do this job. That is the number worth sitting with. You are not fishing in a big pond and picking badly — the pond is small, most of them already have jobs, and the two you want are almost certainly working for somebody else this morning.

One firm is doing this properly. Everyone else is posting and hoping.

Tatro publishes the job, the benefits and an application form, and runs a registered apprenticeship programme. Every other firm around you, including your direct rival in Scott City, has nothing. Being the second firm in the area with a proper careers page is a small piece of work with an open field in front of it.

Finding 4 — The Job Boards Are Thin, So The Advert Has To Go To Them
HVAC technician jobs currently advertised: Garden City, KS 4 Dodge City, KS 2 Hays, KS 2 Sheet metal installer jobs, whole of Kansas: 13
Almost nobody is advertising, and almost nobody is looking

A handful of adverts across the region means the technicians you want are not browsing job boards. They are working for somebody else and not looking at all. Reaching them means putting the job in front of people who already do this work in western Kansas, whether or not they are searching — and giving them a page worth reading when they land.

Finding 5 — What You Have That You Are Not Using
Proof a technician actually cares about
  • Trading since 1977, incorporated 1988. Nearly fifty years. For somebody deciding whether to leave a job, that is stability, and it is nowhere on the site.
  • Bryant Factory Authorized Dealer. Manufacturer backing, proper equipment, real training.
  • Your own photo of the shop, the vans and the crew. It is the only genuine photograph on the whole website — everything else is the manufacturer's stock library. That one picture is worth more to a hire than all of it.
Small fixes while you are here
  • One of your two phone links is written incorrectly and may not dial on some phones. The (620) number works; the 800 number link is malformed.
  • Your email address is plain text, so it cannot be tapped.
  • There is no form anywhere on the site, for customers or applicants.
  • Nothing is measured — no analytics of any kind, so you cannot see what the site does.
  • Leftover demo images sit in the site's media library from the template it was built on.

The Method, In Four Steps
  • Decide the number first. $18 to $40 an hour is what your market is paying. Pick where you sit and say it.
  • Build the page. The work, the truck, the shop, the crew, the pay and what a week actually looks like.
  • Let the questions do the sorting. Experience, EPA card, driver's licence, when they can start — answered before anyone takes your time.
  • Go to them, not to the job boards. Put it in front of technicians already working in western Kansas, whether or not they are looking.

You do not need more applicants. You need the two who can do the job.

One page, the right questions, and the advert pointed at people who already do this work.
